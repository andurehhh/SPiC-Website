Lesson 5: Intellectual Property Rights

Guarding the Created Mind

Moral and Economic Safeguards for Original Ideas and Inventions

Defining Intellectual Property Rights

Intellectual Property Rights (IPRs) are rights established to safeguard the moral and economic entitlements of an individual or group for their original ideas, inventions, or creative expressions.

Intellectual Property (IP) itself is composed of creations, including but not limited to inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, pictures, and computer code. Historically, value was only attributed to tangible and material property. However, the intrinsic value of intangible properties was later recognized because of their significant ability to satisfy the needs of users and society.

The Spectrum of Protection

Different creations require different types of protection. The legal system categorizes these to ensure fair credit and commercial control.

01

Copyright

This is a legal term used to describe the rights that creators have over their literary and artistic works. This protection covers books, music, paintings, sculpture, films, and specifically in the IT field, computer programs, databases, and advertisements.

02

Patent

A patent is an exclusive right granted for an invention. This can be a product or a process that provides a new way of doing something or offers a new technical solution to a specific problem.

03

Trademark

A trademark is a sign capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one enterprise from those of other enterprises. It serves as a vital tool for brand identity and consumer protection in the marketplace.

🎨
Industrial Design

Protects the aesthetic or ornamental aspect of an article, focusing on its visual appeal.

📍
Geographical Indication

Signs used for products with a specific geographical origin and qualities based on that place.

📟
Layout Design

Protects the original topographies and layouts of integrated circuits.

🤫
Trade Secret

Confidential business information which provides a company with a competitive edge.

Personality Rights

Personality rights are a set of legal protections that give individuals control over their name, image, likeness, and other personal characteristics. This is divided into two primary aspects:

Right of Publicity

Focuses on the commercial aspect, allowing individuals to control how their identity is used in endorsements, advertising, and merchandising.

Right of Privacy

Protects individuals from unauthorized intrusions into their personal lives and guards against defamation or harmful depictions.

Why Does IPR Matter?

Protecting intellectual property is essential for fostering a culture of innovation and respect.

Unauthorized Use Protection

Vital for protecting individuals from having their identity or creations exploited for commercial gain without their explicit consent.

Preserving Reputation

In a hyperconnected world, these rights help guard against false or harmful depictions that could tarnish a public image instantly.

Commercial Opportunities

Enable individuals to turn their personal brand and original ideas into valuable commercial assets through sponsorships and collaborations.
